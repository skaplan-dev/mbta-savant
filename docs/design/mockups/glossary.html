Stat Glossary

Every MBTA train car and bus gets a stat line here, built nightly from the T’s own arrival and departure records. This page explains what each number means and how cars are ranked.

How rankings work

Every car is a player. When a six-car train makes a trip, all six cars get credit for it. Over a season, cars get coupled into many different trains, so their own tendencies show through.

Percentiles compare a car with its own line. Red Line cars are ranked against Red Line cars, and buses against buses. Green Line branches are pooled because the same cars run on all of them. Mattapan’s handful of trolleys get stats but aren’t ranked.

POOR55095GREAT

A 95 means the car beat 95% of qualified cars on its line. Red is good and blue is not, whatever the stat.

Qualifying. A car needs enough trips in the season to be ranked: at least 50, and at least a quarter of what a typical car on its line ran. Cars below that still get a page, just without percentile bubbles.

Married pairs. Many rapid-transit cars are permanently coupled in pairs, like 1902 and 1903. They always run together, so they share identical stats.

Fair comparisons. A car’s times are always compared on the same stretch of track, in the same direction, at the same time of day and day of week. A car that works rush hours isn’t penalized for traffic it can’t avoid. Delays longer than five minutes count as five minutes, so one bad breakdown doesn’t decide a season.

Performance

Time Saved▲ HIGHER IS BETTER

Seconds this car saves on each stop-to-stop hop compared with the average car on its line.

For every hop, we compare the car’s time with the typical time for that stretch, direction, hour and day type, then average across the season. The result is shown relative to the line’s average car, so about half of all cars are positive.

+2.9 s means this car is about 3 seconds quicker per stop than the average car
Pace+▲ HIGHER IS BETTER

Speed on a 100 scale: 100 is the line’s average car.

Total expected travel time divided by the car’s actual travel time, centered so the average car is 100. Like baseball’s “plus” stats, 110 is 10% better than average and 90 is 10% worse.

103 means 3% quicker than the average car on the same trips
On-Schedule %▲ HIGHER IS BETTER

Share of stop-to-stop hops the car completed at or under the MBTA’s scheduled time.

Uses the T’s own scheduled travel time for each hop. Schedules are published in whole minutes, so most short hops count as on schedule. The spread between cars is small but real.

Consistency▼ SMALLER SPREAD IS BETTER

How much this car’s hop times vary from trip to trip.

The spread of the middle half of the car’s hop times, after adjusting each hop for its stretch and time of day. A 6 s spread means most hops land within a few seconds of what you’d expect.

Door Time▼ QUICKER IS BETTER

Time stopped at stations compared with a typical stop at the same station and hour.

The car’s median dwell minus the typical dwell. Door time depends on crowds and operators as much as on the car, so take it as a fun stat, not a verdict.

“2 s quick” means it usually leaves 2 seconds sooner than a typical stop

Workload

Days in Service▲ HIGHER IS BETTER

Share of service days this car carried riders.

Only days when the line ran and reported data count, so a system outage doesn’t hurt anyone. A low number usually means time in the shop, or a car held as a spare.

Iron Horse Streak▲ HIGHER IS BETTER

Longest run of consecutive service days in service this season.

Named for the baseball nickname for players who never miss a game.

Miles Logged▲ HIGHER IS BETTER

Distance carried in passenger service this season.

Station-to-station distances added up over every hop. Rail only for now, because bus stop-to-stop distances aren’t in the data yet.

Fun facts (not ranked)

Cruise SpeedNOT RANKED

A fast-but-typical speed between stations: the 90th percentile of the car’s hop speeds.

This mostly reflects the line’s longest, fastest stretches, which every car on the line runs, so it says more about the track than the car.

Avg CrowdingNOT RANKED

Average share of capacity, from on-board passenger counters.

Only newer cars (such as the Red and Orange Line CRRC cars) report it, so it would be unfair to rank.

Coupled WithNOT RANKED

The cars this car most often runs coupled to, and how many trips they shared.

A car’s married partner shows up at 100%.

Routes RunBUS ONLY

How many different routes a bus ran this season. The utility players of the fleet.
